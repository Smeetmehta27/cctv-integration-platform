1. CHALLENGE OVERVIEW
============================================================

The challenge is to design and build a secure, scalable, interoperable, technically feasible, and cost-effective platform for integrating CCTV infrastructure deployed across Gujarat.

The challenge is driven by the fact that multiple Government Departments currently operate independent CCTV systems. These systems are heterogeneous: they may use different camera types, vendors, Video Management Systems (VMS), storage technologies, formats, architectures, connectivity mechanisms, retention policies, and feed-sharing protocols.

The intended solution should make maximum practical use of existing infrastructure rather than assuming that all existing CCTV hardware and departmental systems will be replaced.

The high-level objective is to move from fragmented departmental CCTV ecosystems toward a unified video management, integration, analytics, and operational ecosystem while preserving security, interoperability, scalability, and future extensibility.

The problem explicitly recognizes that CCTV assets are spread across geographically dispersed locations and departments, with distances extending to approximately 1,000 km across the State.

The challenge also expects integration with existing Government databases containing law-enforcement-relevant records so that CCTV detections can be correlated with structured watchlists and used to generate real-time alerts and actionable information.

------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 BACKGROUND
------------------------------------------------------------

At present, 26 different Government Departments are operating independent CCTV systems across the State.

These systems include both analog and IP-based cameras deployed at geographically dispersed locations ranging from border districts to areas such as Valsad, Dahod, Somnath, Jamnagar, and Dwarka.

Each department currently operates a standalone camera ecosystem.

Some departments use cloud-based storage solutions, while others rely on local storage infrastructure.

Video retention periods also vary significantly:
- Some systems store footage for 7 days.
- Other systems store footage for 15 days or more.

Camera usage and deployment patterns differ among departments based on operational requirements and functional objectives.

Examples given in the challenge:

A) Home Department:
- Cameras are primarily deployed in public domains.
- Purposes include traffic monitoring.
- Purposes also include law & order management.
- Cameras are also used for crime detection.

B) Food & Civil Supplies Department:
- Cameras are mainly installed at godowns.
- Cameras are installed at PDS shops.
- Cameras are installed at related facilities.

C) RTO:
- Cameras are deployed at offices.
- Cameras are used for testing tracks.
- Cameras are used at checkpoints.
- Cameras are used at other operational locations.

The Government intends to integrate government cameras, primarily those deployed in the public domain, into a unified video management and analytics ecosystem.

The proposed solution should also support viewing capabilities for public-facing CCTV cameras installed by:
- Societies
- Malls
- Commercial establishments
- Other private entities

Such private/publicly facing CCTV integration is expected wherever technically feasible and legally permitted.

2.2 GOVERNMENT DATABASE INTEGRATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Several Government departments already maintain critical databases.

The challenge specifically names:
- VAHAN
- SARTHI
- eGujCop (Gujarat Police's CCTNS platform)
- AFIS
- NAFIS

These databases contain or support records such as:
- Arrested persons
- Stolen vehicles
- Wanted criminals
- Missing persons
- Unidentified dead bodies
- Fingerprint data

The proposed CCTV Integration System should be integrated with these databases so that law-enforcement agencies can obtain:
- Automated real-time alerts
- Proactive monitoring capabilities
- Correlation of CCTV detections with relevant records

------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 ARCHITECTURAL EXPECTATION
------------------------------------------------------------

The system should be designed with a:
- Scalable architecture
- Modular architecture
- Future-ready architecture

It should support:
- Seamless integration
- Secure feed exchange
- Standardised integration mechanisms
- Scalability
- Compatibility

The expected direction is not simply "centralize everything." The architecture should intelligently handle existing departmental systems, interoperability, security, distributed infrastructure, analytics requirements, and future onboarding.

------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 CORE GOAL
------------------------------------------------------------

The challenge's core goal is:

Propose a secure, scalable, interoperable, technically feasible, and cost-effective approach that uses existing infrastructure to the maximum practical extent.

This implies that the solution should:
- Reuse existing cameras and infrastructure wherever possible.
- Avoid unnecessary replacement of working departmental systems.
- Support multiple vendors and VMS platforms.
- Preserve departmental operational continuity.
- Provide a path toward unified monitoring and analytics.
- Provide a realistic statewide scale strategy.

------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 KEY CHALLENGES
------------------------------------------------------------

CHALLENGE 01 — HETEROGENEOUS INFRASTRUCTURE
Different vendors, VMS platforms, AMC periods, storage architectures, camera types, formats, and feed-sharing protocols must be handled.

Implication:
The system must be vendor-neutral and integration-friendly, with adapters/connectors and/or standard protocols rather than relying on a single vendor ecosystem.

CHALLENGE 02 — GEOGRAPHICAL DISPERSION
Camera sites are distributed across the State, with distances extending to approximately 1,000 kilometers.

Implication:
Networking, latency, regional/edge deployment, bandwidth optimization, failover, and centralized-versus-distributed processing are critical architecture considerations.

CHALLENGE 03 — UNIFIED ANALYTICS
The system should support analytics and event handling across onboarded cameras through a unified framework.

Implication:
Analytics should be designed as modular services that can consume streams or metadata from multiple sources and produce standardized, searchable events.

CHALLENGE 04 — SCALABILITY
New cameras, departments, systems, and future analytics should be onboarded without major redesign.

The eventual target discussed throughout the challenge is approximately 80,000 cameras.

Implication:
The solution should demonstrate a credible scaling path rather than only a small proof of concept.

5. MODEL 2 — UNIFIED VIEWING & METADATA ANALYTICS MODEL
============================================================

TITLE:
Unified Viewing & Metadata Analytics Model

SUBTITLE:
Centralised Viewing with Metadata-Based Analytics

------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 INTRODUCTION
------------------------------------------------------------

Multiple departments currently operate independent CCTV systems through their own Video Management Systems (VMS).

Central command centres are required to access these feeds through multiple separate viewer systems, which increases operational complexity and reduces monitoring efficiency.

Under Model 2, a unified viewing platform is proposed.

Through this platform:
- CCTV feeds from different departmental systems can be accessed through a single interface.
- Existing departmental infrastructure is not disturbed.
- Existing departmental VMS/storage systems continue to operate independently.

The proposed unified viewing platform connects directly to each departmental CCTV or VMS system through:
- RTSP
- ONVIF
- Vendor SDKs
- Available APIs

The platform integrates accessible video streams into a single interface without introducing an intermediate middleware or federation layer.

This model primarily focuses on:
- Centralised viewing
- Operational accessibility
- Generation of selective metadata/analytics from accessible video streams

Depending on technical feasibility, capabilities may include:
- ANPR-based metadata generation
- Event tagging
- Camera-wise indexing
- Searchable vehicle movement records

The model does not require centralised storage of all video feeds.

6. MODEL 3 — VMS FEDERATION & MIDDLEWARE INTEGRATION MODEL
============================================================

TITLE:
VMS Federation & Middleware Integration Model

SUBTITLE:
Interoperability & Cross-System Integration Layer

------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 INTRODUCTION
------------------------------------------------------------

Different departments across the State use heterogeneous CCTV systems supplied by multiple vendors, resulting in:
- Fragmented monitoring infrastructure
- Interoperability challenges

Under Model 3, a middleware/federation layer is proposed.

The middleware layer integrates multiple departmental VMS platforms through:
- APIs
- SDKs
- Metadata exchange
- Event sharing mechanisms
- Standard protocols

The objective is to enable:
- Interoperability
- Cross-platform communication
- Centralized event correlation
- Unified operational workflows

This is achieved without replacing existing departmental systems.

Departments can retain:
- Their own infrastructure
- Their own operational control

while enabling:
- Higher-level integration
- Metadata sharing
- Federated analytics
- Coordinated monitoring at the central level

IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE FROM MODEL 2:
Unlike Model 2, the solution does not connect directly to each departmental CCTV or VMS system from the downstream application.

Instead:
- The middleware/federation layer acts as the common integration platform.
- It communicates with multiple existing VMS platforms.
- It exposes a single unified interface for downstream applications, dashboards, and AI services.

42. CRITICAL DIFFERENCES BETWEEN MODELS
============================================================

MODEL 1:
Metadata/registry/GIS.
No centralised live video streaming or recording.

MODEL 2:
Direct unified viewing access to departmental systems using RTSP/ONVIF/vendor APIs.
No requirement for centralised storage of all feeds.
No intermediate middleware/federation layer in the described model.

MODEL 3:
Middleware/federation layer is the common integration point.
Downstream applications do not directly integrate individually with every VMS.
Supports cross-system metadata/event exchange and correlation.

MODEL 4:
Fully centralised VMS.
Centralised monitoring, management, recording, storage, playback and analytics.
Requires robust centralized infrastructure.

HYBRID:
Can combine these approaches and may be the strongest route where a balance between reuse, interoperability, edge/regional processing, central analytics, and cost is required.

43. WHAT THE HACKATHON IS REALLY TESTING
============================================================

At a deeper architectural level, the hackathon is testing whether a team can solve a real-world statewide integration problem rather than merely build a CCTV dashboard.

The solution must deal with five intertwined dimensions:

1. INTEGRATION
How do you connect thousands of heterogeneous systems without replacing everything?

2. VIDEO + METADATA
How do you process massive amounts of video while turning important observations into searchable, useful metadata?

3. OPERATIONAL INTELLIGENCE
How do you turn detections into actionable alerts and movement histories?

4. SCALE
How do you progress from a ~50-camera demonstration to approximately 80,000 cameras?

5. GOVERNANCE & SECURITY
How do you protect access, preserve departmental control, maintain auditability, handle privacy/legal constraints, and keep the system reliable?

44. PRACTICAL DEFINITION OF SUCCESS
============================================================

A solution should be considered aligned with the challenge when a reviewer can observe the following end-to-end story:

"Here are multiple heterogeneous CCTV sources from different departments/vendors. We onboard them into our platform without replacing the underlying systems. The platform knows where every camera is and who owns it. We can view the feeds centrally. AI analyzes relevant streams and converts observations into structured events. Those events are continuously checked against an authorized watchlist. A matching vehicle/entity creates a real-time alert. The operator can inspect the evidence, see where the vehicle was detected previously, see the route on GIS, and search the full event history. The same architecture can expand from the evaluation's approximately 50 cameras to approximately 80,000 cameras through regional/edge processing, scalable infrastructure, distributed storage, secure integration, and horizontal scaling."

That is the operational narrative the architecture, demo, PPT/PDF and HLD should collectively prove.

46. SOURCE-FAITHFUL NOTES / INTERPRETATION GUARDRAILS
============================================================

1. "Approximately 80,000 cameras" is a scale target repeatedly stated in the source. It should not be interpreted as proof that every one of the 80,000 cameras has identical video resolution, frame rate, retention, or streaming characteristics.

2. The challenge describes heterogeneous environments. Therefore, any capacity calculation that assumes one uniform camera profile would be only a planning assumption, not a source fact.

3. Retention periods are explicitly different across departments. The architecture must therefore allow policy differences.

4. Model 1 explicitly does not provide centralized live streaming or recording. It is a metadata/asset visibility foundation.

5. Model 2 is described as direct connection to departmental CCTV/VMS systems through RTSP, ONVIF, vendor SDKs, or APIs, without an intermediate middleware/federation layer.

6. Model 3 introduces middleware/federation and makes it the common integration point.

7. Model 4 is the most centralized and infrastructure-heavy reference model.

8. A hybrid approach is permitted.

9. Existing departmental systems should remain operational and, where feasible, should be reused.

10. Private/public-facing CCTV can be included wherever feasible and legally permitted.

11. The evaluation is strongly demonstration-oriented. A visual mock-up alone is explicitly insufficient.

12. Bonus features do not compensate for failure to satisfy mandatory submission/test requirements.

13. The source mentions "five reference solution models" while the visible material details Model 1, Model 2, Model 3, Model 4, and Hybrid/Innovative Architecture. This document treats those five visible choices as the intended set.
